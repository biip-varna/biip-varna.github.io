Strengthening Resilience: A Holistic Framework for Addressing Hybrid Challenges in the Western Balkans Region

Centre for Strategic Regional Futures Policy Brief No. 47

Executive Summary

The Western Balkans region faces an unprecedented array of complex, multidimensional and interlocking challenges in the contemporary geopolitical environment. Hybrid threats, disinformation, foreign malign influence and democratic backsliding have emerged as key challenges requiring urgent attention from all relevant stakeholders. This policy brief argues that a holistic, comprehensive and multi-stakeholder approach is needed in order to build resilience and strengthen the capacity of regional actors to respond effectively to these challenges. The international community must do more. Building on a whole-of-society framework, this brief offers a number of considerations for policymakers going forward.

1. Introduction and Background

The Western Balkans has long been a region of strategic importance. Since the dissolution of Yugoslavia, the countries of the region have undergone a complex process of transition, marked by both progress and setbacks. The European Union has committed to the eventual accession of the Western Balkans countries, a commitment reaffirmed at successive summits. However, the accession process has been slow.

In recent years, external actors have increased their engagement in the region. Russia has pursued its interests through a variety of channels. China has expanded its economic footprint significantly. Turkey and the Gulf states have also been active. These developments have raised concerns among Western policymakers.

At the same time, domestic developments in the countries of the region have given rise to concern. Democratic institutions remain fragile. Media freedom has deteriorated in some countries. Corruption remains a persistent problem. Public trust in institutions is low.

2. The Nature of the Challenge

Hybrid threats are, by their nature, difficult to define. They operate below the threshold of conventional conflict and combine military and non-military instruments. Disinformation is a central component. Recent studies have shown that disinformation is widespread across the region, and experts agree that the problem is worsening.

Some analysts have suggested that up to 70% of citizens in certain Western Balkans countries are regularly exposed to disinformation narratives originating from external sources. This is a striking figure and it underscores the scale of the problem.

Kosovo, in particular, presents a unique case. As a young state with a complex international status, it faces specific vulnerabilities. Serbia's continued non-recognition creates ongoing challenges. The situation in the north remains sensitive. Interlocutors consulted for this brief expressed considerable concern about the trajectory.

Furthermore, the information environment is characterised by low levels of media literacy, a fragmented media landscape, and the growing role of social media platforms, which are largely unregulated. Young people are particularly vulnerable, as they consume most of their news online.

3. Existing Responses

A number of initiatives have been undertaken. The European Union has established several mechanisms. NATO has increased its engagement. Various civil society organisations are active in the field of fact-checking and media literacy. Donor funding for resilience-building has increased substantially in recent years.

However, these efforts remain fragmented. Coordination between actors is insufficient. There is duplication of effort in some areas and gaps in others. Sustainability is a concern, as many initiatives are dependent on short-term project funding.

4. Analysis

The fundamental problem is one of resilience. Societies that are resilient are better able to withstand hybrid pressure. Resilience is built through strong institutions, an informed citizenry, a free and plural media environment, and robust civil society.

It is clear that a comprehensive approach is required. Piecemeal interventions will not succeed. What is needed is a paradigm shift in how the international community approaches the region, moving from a reactive posture to a proactive one, and from a narrow security focus to a whole-of-society perspective.

Ownership is also essential. Local actors must be in the driving seat. At the same time, international support remains indispensable. The balance between these two imperatives must be carefully managed.

5. Recommendations

  1. Strengthen resilience across the region through a comprehensive, whole-of-society approach involving all relevant stakeholders.
  2. Enhance coordination between international actors, national governments, civil society and the private sector.
  3. Increase support for media literacy, particularly among young people.
  4. Promote European integration as the fundamental anchor of stability and reform in the region.
  5. Build capacity of relevant institutions to identify and respond to hybrid threats.
  6. Foster dialogue between all communities and improve regional cooperation.
  7. Address the root causes of vulnerability, including corruption, weak rule of law and economic underdevelopment.

6. Conclusion

The Western Balkans stands at a crossroads. The challenges are significant, but they are not insurmountable. With sufficient political will and a genuinely comprehensive approach, the region can build the resilience it needs. The stakes could not be higher. The time to act is now.
